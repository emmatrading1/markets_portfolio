# Market Pulse 2026-03-27

Driver  
Markets are driven by a persistent inflation–energy narrative as the Iran conflict keeps oil elevated while supply disruption risk remains unresolved. The focus is shifting from short-term geopolitical headlines to the durability of the shock, with investors increasingly pricing higher-for-longer inflation and rates. Political attempts to cap oil prices add instability, reinforcing a fragile and headline-driven macro environment.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 4.02% (+4bp), US 10Y 4.46% (+5bp) — yields move higher with a bear steepening, reflecting rising inflation expectations and term premium rather than a clean repricing of near-term Fed policy.<br>  
USD: DXY 100.01 (+0.15%) — dollar strengthens modestly, supported by higher yields and macro uncertainty.<br>  
FX: EURUSD −0.06%, USDJPY +0.08%, USDCNH −0.03% — FX remains relatively muted; USDJPY follows yields higher, while CNH stability suggests no immediate China-specific stress.<br>  
Equities: SPX −1.74%, VIX 28.42 (+3.57%) — clear risk-off tone, with equities down and volatility rising toward more cautious levels.<br>  
Commodities: Oil 96.37 (+2.74%), Gold +1.13% — oil continues to price supply disruption risk, while gold rises alongside, showing demand for hedging despite higher yields.

WSJ/FT Insights  
The market is increasingly anchored to oil as the central macro variable. FT highlights that energy-driven inflation could push US inflation significantly higher (OECD ~4.2%), reinforcing that the shock is not transitory. At the same time, oil pricing is heavily influenced by political reaction functions, with US rhetoric acting as a temporary cap but not removing underlying supply risk. This creates a regime where inflation expectations, yields and volatility remain unstable and policy-sensitive.

Risk  
If oil continues to move higher or supply disruption through the Strait of Hormuz intensifies, inflation expectations could reprice further, pushing yields higher — especially on the long end — and pressuring equities while keeping volatility elevated. Conversely, any credible easing in geopolitical risk would likely trigger a joint decline in oil and yields, but current positioning suggests the market is not yet ready to price a clean normalization scenario.
