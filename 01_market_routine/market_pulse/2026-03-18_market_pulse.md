# Market Pulse 2026-03-18

Driver  
Oil supply disruption remains the dominant macro driver as the closure of the Strait of Hormuz continues to constrain global energy flows. Physical oil markets are experiencing acute scarcity, with substitute crude grades and transport costs surging, while futures remain relatively anchored, highlighting a growing dislocation between physical and paper markets. Elevated oil prices are reinforcing inflation risks and delaying expectations of monetary easing.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.708 (+3bp), US 10Y 4.214 (+1bp) — front-end yields rise more than the long end, leading to a bear flattening as markets reprice higher policy rates in response to persistent inflation pressures.<br>
USD: DXY 99.79 (+0.23%) — the dollar strengthens modestly, supported by higher yields and relatively tighter US financial conditions.<br>
FX: EURUSD −0.20%, USDJPY +0.24%, USDCNH +0.05% — broad USD strength consistent with widening rate differentials and stable global risk sentiment.<br>

Commodities: Oil 96.16 (+0.16%), Gold −2.57% — oil remains elevated amid supply constraints, while gold declines as higher real yields and a stronger dollar weigh on safe-haven demand.

FT Insights  
Economists increasingly warn that sustained oil prices near $100 could weaken US growth while keeping inflation elevated, forcing the Federal Reserve to delay rate cuts. Markets are shifting toward a “higher for longer” rate environment, reflecting the risk that energy-driven inflation persists even as economic activity slows.

Risk  
A prolonged disruption in oil flows could push energy prices materially higher, reinforcing inflation pressures and forcing further tightening in financial conditions. This would increase the risk of a policy-induced slowdown, with higher front-end yields, a stronger dollar, and growing stress across risk assets and credit markets.
