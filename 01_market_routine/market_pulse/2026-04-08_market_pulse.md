# Market Pulse 2026-04-08

Driver  
Markets are repricing a sharp de-escalation in geopolitical risk following a potential Iran ceasefire, with the main driver being the collapse in oil prices as supply disruption fears around the Strait of Hormuz unwind.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.72 (−8bp), US 10Y 4.24 (−6bp) — bull flattening led by the front-end, suggesting reduced policy pressure and softer rate expectations.<br>
USD: DXY 98.84 (−0.68%) — dollar weakens below 100 as safe-haven demand fades.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.80%, USDJPY −0.82%, USDCNH −0.44% — USD broadly softer, with JPY strengthening in line with lower yields.<br>
Equities: SPX +0.08%, VIX 20.73 (−19.6%) — sharp drop in volatility reflects a compression of geopolitical risk premium.<br>
Commodities: Oil 96.10 (−12.9%), Gold +2.26% — oil collapse dominates the move, removing immediate inflation pressure, while gold remains supported by underlying flows.

WSJ/FT Insights  
FT highlights that the ceasefire announcement triggered a rapid unwind of energy-driven risk, with oil dropping sharply as markets reprice supply risks. At the same time, central bank behavior is becoming a key driver in gold markets, with Turkey selling reserves to support its currency, illustrating how liquidity needs can override traditional safe-haven dynamics.

Risk  
Markets are now heavily positioned for de-escalation. Any reversal in tensions could lead to a sharp rebound in oil and volatility. If easing holds, focus is likely to shift back toward growth and monetary policy, with lower yields signaling a softer macro regime.
