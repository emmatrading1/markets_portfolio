# Market Pulse 2026-04-03

Driver  
Markets are back in a clearer inflation-shock regime, with oil surging sharply and rates moving higher again as the Iran conflict starts to transmit more visibly into the real economy. The focus is no longer only geopolitical risk, but the growing stagflationary impact of sustained energy disruption.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.85 (+5bp), US 10Y 4.37 (+5bp) — yields move higher with a slight bear steepening, showing renewed inflation and term-premium repricing as oil pushes further above $100.<br>
USD: DXY 100.15 (+0.60%) — dollar strengthens back above 100, supported by higher yields and renewed macro uncertainty.<br>
FX: EURUSD −0.54%, USDJPY +0.57%, USDCNH +0.31% — FX aligns with a firmer dollar; moves remain orderly but clearly USD-positive.<br>
Equities: SPX +0.72%, VIX 26.37 (+7.46%) — equities remain resilient, but higher VIX shows investors are still paying for protection, pointing to a mixed but cautious risk backdrop.<br>
Commodities: Oil 107.10 (+8.29%), Gold −2.84% — oil is the dominant macro signal, while gold falls as higher yields and a stronger dollar outweigh safe-haven demand.

WSJ/FT Insights  
FT points to a more visible stagflation transmission: airlines are cutting capacity, fuel shortages are becoming operational rather than hypothetical, and energy security is now driving policy and trade behavior across regions such as Asia and Australia. This supports the idea that oil is no longer just a market headline but a real-economy shock feeding inflation concerns and higher long-end yields.

Risk  
If oil keeps accelerating and the supply shock broadens further into transport and industry, markets could reprice stagflation more aggressively through higher yields, stronger USD and renewed equity weakness. If instead growth fears start to dominate again, the current bear move in rates could reverse quickly, but for now oil remains the clearest driver.
