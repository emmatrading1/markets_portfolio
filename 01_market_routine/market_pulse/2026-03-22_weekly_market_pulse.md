Week of Mar 17–21, 2026

The dominant theme this week shifted from geopolitical uncertainty to a persistent energy-driven inflation shock. Markets are no longer treating the disruption as temporary, but are increasingly pricing a scenario where supply constraints last and feed into broader inflation expectations.

Energy

The severity of the shock became clearer as stress intensified in physical markets. While Brent remained around $100–110, Middle Eastern benchmarks surged significantly higher, highlighting acute tightness in Gulf-origin supply.

Disruptions are now extending beyond shipping routes to infrastructure, suggesting supply constraints could last longer than initially expected. Across Asia, governments have already implemented rationing, subsidies and demand controls, signalling a transition from precaution to active crisis management.

Rates

Rates markets led the repricing. Short-end yields moved sharply higher as investors priced out rate cuts and began to consider renewed tightening driven by energy inflation.

The UK saw the most pronounced reaction, with gilt yields reaching their highest level since 2008. Markets are now pricing multiple rate hikes this year, a sharp reversal from prior expectations.

The broader dynamic reflects a feedback loop between markets and central banks, with rising yields tightening financial conditions ahead of actual policy moves.

Equities and volatility

Equity markets remained fragile, with higher yields and inflation concerns weighing on valuations. Direction was inconsistent, with brief rebounds fading as macro risks re-emerged.

Volatility stayed elevated, particularly across rates and commodities, suggesting continued cross-asset instability.

FX

The US dollar remained supported by higher yields and risk uncertainty. FX moves were relatively contained compared to commodities and rates, though energy-importing economies showed signs of pressure.

What to watch next week

Focus will shift to the duration of the shock and its transmission into inflation. Markets will monitor signs of sustained supply disruption, demand destruction in Asia, and whether long-term inflation expectations begin to move higher.

A key risk is a further extension of the move from short-term rates into the long end of the curve, which would signal a more structural shift in the macro environment.
