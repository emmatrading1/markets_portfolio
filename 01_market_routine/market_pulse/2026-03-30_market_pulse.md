# Market Pulse 2026-03-30

Driver  
Markets are driven by a growth-risk readjustment on top of the energy shock, as oil pushes back above $100 while yields slip and equities remain under pressure. The conflict is now being priced less as a short-term headline event and more as a prolonged disruption with downside risks to growth alongside persistent inflation pressure.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.89 (−2bp), US 10Y 4.40 (−3bp) — yields move lower with a slight bull steepening, suggesting growth fears are starting to outweigh the pure inflation repricing at the margin.<br>
USD: DXY 100.26 (+0.07%) — dollar stays firm above 100, supported by uncertainty and safe-haven demand.<br>
FX: EURUSD −0.14%, USDJPY −0.42%, USDCNH −0.04% — FX remains relatively muted; JPY strengthens on haven demand, while CNH stays broadly stable.<br>
Equities: SPX −1.67%, VIX 31.09 (+0.13%) — equities stay under pressure and volatility remains above 30, pointing to a clearer risk-off tone.<br>
Commodities: Oil 101.84 (+0.66%), Gold +0.86% — oil remains elevated on supply disruption risk, while gold rises as hedging demand offsets dollar strength.

WSJ/FT Insights  
Oil remains the key macro driver, but the market is increasingly pricing the duration of the shock rather than only the headline itself. FT highlights growing concern that a longer conflict, tighter alternative shipping routes and fiscal strain in Europe could deepen the hit to growth while keeping inflation elevated. That combination explains why oil and gold are higher, equities weaker, and yields slightly lower rather than continuing a clean inflation-led sell-off.

Risk  
If the conflict extends further and disruption through Hormuz and alternative routes worsens, oil could move higher again and revive the inflation shock narrative more aggressively. But if growth fears continue to build faster than inflation expectations, yields may keep falling even with energy prices elevated, reinforcing a stagflationary market tone rather than a simple higher-for-longer rates story.
