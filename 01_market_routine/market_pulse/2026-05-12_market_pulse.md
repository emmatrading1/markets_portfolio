# Market Pulse 2026-05-12

Driver  
Oil is still the dominant driver. Crude moved back above $100 after Trump rejected Iran’s latest response, while Aramco warned that fuel inventories are tightening quickly. But despite higher oil and rising inflation fears, equities continue to hold up and volatility remains relatively contained.

FX: EURUSD -0.30%, USDJPY +0.30%, USDCNH flat — USDJPY still tracking higher US yields, broader FX relatively calm.  
Equities: SPX +0.19%, VIX 18.8 (+2.3%) — equities still resilient even with oil above $100, while VIX remains below stress territory.  
Commodities: Oil 101.0 (+2.8%), Gold -0.65% — oil higher on failed peace momentum, gold weaker as yields rise.

WSJ/FT Insights  
The market is transitioning from a pure oil shock into a broader inflation and supply-chain story. Aramco’s warning about critically low fuel inventories suggests the next phase may be tighter refined fuel markets rather than just higher crude prices.

Another important development is the spillover into agriculture and biofuels. Hedge funds are increasingly positioning in soyabean oil and corn because high energy prices are starting to affect the entire commodity complex.

At the same time, markets still do not look genuinely defensive. Equities remain firm, VIX is below 20, and investors continue buying risk despite higher yields and oil.

Risk  
The risk is that markets become too comfortable with the energy shock. Rates are clearly pricing inflation persistence and higher-for-longer policy, but equities are still assuming growth and earnings can absorb it. If oil remains above $100 for several more weeks, inflation pressure could eventually become harder for equities to ignore.
