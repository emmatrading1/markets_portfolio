# Market Pulse 2026-03-31

Driver  
Markets are transitioning from a pure inflation shock narrative to a clearer growth-risk repricing, as the Iran conflict extends and begins to weigh on global demand expectations. Oil remains elevated, but the focus is shifting toward the economic damage from sustained high energy prices.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.83 (−1bp), US 10Y 4.33 (−2bp) — yields move lower with a slight bull steepening, indicating growth concerns are increasingly dominating over inflation fears.<br>
USD: DXY 100.47 (−0.02%) — dollar remains stable above 100, still supported by uncertainty but showing no additional momentum.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.03%, USDJPY −0.01%, USDCNH −0.04% — FX largely unchanged; JPY marginally stronger in line with lower yields, overall low conviction.<br>
Equities: SPX −0.39%, VIX 28.85 (−5.75%) — mild risk-off tone in equities, but volatility easing slightly, suggesting no escalation in stress.<br>
Commodities: Oil 103.79 (−1.15%), Gold +1.03% — oil remains elevated despite slight pullback, while gold gains on hedging demand.

WSJ/FT Insights  
The narrative is shifting toward growth deterioration as the conflict drags on. FT highlights that investors are beginning to move into duration, reflecting concerns that sustained high energy prices will erode real income and slow global activity. At the same time, foreign central banks selling Treasuries adds structural pressure to the rates market, complicating the usual safe-haven dynamics.

Risk  
If growth concerns continue to build, yields could move lower despite elevated oil prices, reinforcing a stagflationary environment. However, persistent energy disruption or renewed escalation could quickly shift the market back toward an inflation-dominated regime, pushing yields higher again and increasing cross-asset volatility.
