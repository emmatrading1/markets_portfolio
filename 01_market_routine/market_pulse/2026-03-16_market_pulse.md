# Market Pulse 2026-03-16

Driver  
Markets are partially unwinding last week’s geopolitical and inflation premium as oil prices retreat and investors reassess the probability of a sustained disruption to flows through the Strait of Hormuz. While the conflict remains unresolved, today’s price action reflects a temporary easing in immediate energy shock fears.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.694 (−0.94%), US 10Y 4.234 (−1.14%) — Treasury yields decline across the curve as investors rotate back into duration and unwind part of the inflation premium priced earlier in the week.<br>
USD: DXY 99.856 (−0.63%) — the dollar weakens as falling yields reduce support for US assets.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.73%, USDJPY −0.34%, USDCNH −0.24% — broad USD softness across major pairs as lower yields and improving risk sentiment support other currencies.<br>
Equities: SPX +1.14%, VIX 24.19 (−11.03%) — equities rebound while volatility drops sharply as investors unwind hedges built during last week’s geopolitical stress.<br>
Commodities: Oil 95.46 (−3.87%), Gold −0.26% — oil gives back part of its geopolitical risk premium while gold slips slightly as safe-haven demand eases.

FT Insights  
Central banks now face a more complex backdrop as the conflict raises the risk of renewed commodity-driven inflation. Policymakers are unlikely to respond immediately, but rising energy prices could delay expected rate cuts if the shock proves persistent.

Risk  
If disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz intensifies again, oil could quickly move back toward $100, forcing markets to reprice inflation expectations and pushing yields and volatility higher.
