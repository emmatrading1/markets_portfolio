# Weekly Market Pulse — Week of May 11–17, 2026

The main story this week was the shift from an oil shock to a rates shock.

Oil stayed elevated above $100, but the bigger move happened in global bond markets as investors started pricing the risk that inflation may stay high for longer.

Energy

Oil stayed high all week.

Hormuz disruption still matters, inventories are tightening, and the market is becoming more worried about prolonged supply stress rather than a short-term spike.

WTI finished around $105, Brent around $109.

Rates

Global yields moved sharply higher.

US long-end yields hit the highest levels since 2007, while Japanese yields reached record highs as markets increasingly price persistent inflation and fewer chances of rate cuts.

The BOJ also became a bigger story because higher Japanese yields raise the risk of capital moving back into Japan from US Treasuries.

Equities and volatility

Equities held up relatively well early in the week, but pressure started building into Friday.

VIX moved back toward 18 and markets became less comfortable with the combination of:
• high oil  
• higher real yields  
• tighter financial conditions

FX

Dollar strengthened again as yields moved higher.

USDJPY stayed important because of BOJ expectations and repatriation risk, while CNH weakness reflected broader China and growth concerns.

Macro / flows

The inflation shock is spreading globally.

China warned about imported inflation, the Bank of Canada discussed possible hikes again, and markets are increasingly focused on whether central banks can still sound dovish with oil above $100.

The important shift is that this is no longer just an oil story — it is becoming a broader inflation, rates and liquidity story.

What to watch next week

• Oil above or below $100  
• Fed minutes and rate expectations  
• Global bond yields  
• JGB volatility and yen strength  
• Consumer weakness from higher fuel costs  
• Whether equities start reacting harder to rates
