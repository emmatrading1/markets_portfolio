# Market Pulse 2026-04-02

Driver  
Markets show a partial stabilization after recent stress, with a modest risk-on tone emerging despite the ongoing energy shock. The focus remains on growth risks, but today’s price action suggests a temporary easing in market tension rather than a resolution of the underlying macro narrative.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.81 (+2bp), US 10Y 4.33 (+1bp) — yields move higher with a slight bear flattening, indicating a mild repricing of short-term policy expectations rather than a shift in long-term growth outlook.<br>
USD: DXY 99.60 (−0.26%) — dollar weakens, reflecting reduced immediate demand for safety.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.28%, USDJPY +0.09%, USDCNH −0.14% — FX shows a softer USD environment; EUR benefits from dollar pullback, moves remain contained overall.<br>
Equities: SPX +0.72%, VIX 24.54 (−2.81%) — equities rebound and volatility declines, pointing to a temporary improvement in risk sentiment.<br>
Commodities: Oil 99.20 (−2.31%), Gold +2.10% — oil pulls back but remains near key levels, while gold rises sharply, reflecting continued hedging demand.

WSJ/FT Insights  
FT highlights that central banks are increasingly concerned about a stagflationary setup, where the energy shock simultaneously pressures growth and inflation. At the same time, divergence in policy responses is emerging: Europe signals readiness to react if inflation persists, while the Fed remains in wait-and-see mode. Structural dynamics also matter, with China appearing relatively insulated and offering an alternative safe-haven bond market, contrasting with ongoing stress in developed sovereign debt.

Risk  
The key risk is that today’s stabilization proves temporary. If the conflict persists and energy prices re-accelerate, markets could quickly revert to a more defensive regime with higher yields and renewed equity pressure. Conversely, if growth concerns dominate further, the move into duration could resume, pushing yields lower even in the presence of elevated inflation risk.
