Week of Mar 24–28, 2026

The dominant theme this week confirmed a transition from geopolitical uncertainty to a persistent, supply-driven energy shock. Markets are no longer pricing a temporary disruption, but a prolonged constraint feeding into inflation, term premium and cross-asset instability.

Energy

The shock deepened as physical constraints intensified. Brent remained elevated around ~$105–110, but the key development was the widening impact beyond oil into shipping and fuel logistics. Bunker fuel prices surged and key hubs such as Fujairah were effectively shut, forcing ships to reroute and even prioritize fuel transport over cargo.

This highlights a shift from price volatility to real supply chain disruption, reinforcing the idea that the shock is structural and spreading across global trade.

Rates

Rates continued to reprice higher, with both short and long-end yields elevated. While short-end moves reflect reduced expectations of rate cuts, the long end is increasingly driven by higher term premium and uncertainty rather than pure inflation expectations.

The key shift is that bonds are losing their diversification role, requiring investors to demand higher yields to hold duration.

Equities and volatility

Equity markets remained volatile and fragile, with sharp drawdowns followed by unstable rebounds. The S&P 500 reached multi-month lows during the week before partial recoveries.

Volatility stayed elevated, reflecting a market struggling to price both inflation risk and growth slowdown simultaneously.

FX

The US dollar remained supported by higher yields and global uncertainty. However, FX moves remained secondary compared to the dominant oil and rates dynamic, with limited conviction outside of energy-driven flows.

What to watch next week

Focus remains on the persistence of the supply shock and its broader transmission. Markets will monitor developments around the Strait of Hormuz, escalation versus negotiation signals, and further spillovers into shipping, fuel logistics and global supply chains.

A key risk is a continued rise in term premium and long-end yields, which would reinforce the breakdown of diversification and keep cross-asset volatility elevated.
