Week of Mar 10–14, 2026

The dominant theme this week remained the Middle East energy shock, but markets increasingly shifted from the immediate geopolitical story toward its macro consequences. Oil prices experienced extreme volatility as traders reacted to conflicting signals about the duration of disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

After briefly spiking above $100 earlier in the crisis, crude retraced sharply before stabilising around the low $90s. The violent swings reflected a market struggling to price the probability of a sustained supply shock versus a temporary geopolitical risk premium.

Energy

Oil markets experienced one of the most volatile weeks in recent years. Prices initially surged on attacks targeting Gulf energy infrastructure and disruptions to tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, a route that normally carries roughly 20% of global oil and LNG supply.

However, crude prices later reversed sharply after signals that tensions could ease and as policymakers discussed potential releases from strategic reserves. Even so, markets remain extremely sensitive to developments in the Gulf, as even partial disruptions could remove several million barrels per day from global supply.

Rates

Bond markets increasingly reflected the inflation implications of higher energy prices. Early in the week yields rose as investors priced stronger inflation risks linked to oil.

Later sessions saw some safe-haven demand for Treasuries as volatility in energy markets intensified. By the end of the week longer-dated yields were again moving higher, suggesting investors are beginning to price a more persistent inflation risk rather than simply short-term geopolitical volatility.

Equities and volatility

Equity markets struggled to establish a clear direction. The S&P 500 initially rebounded as geopolitical fears eased temporarily but later declined again as higher yields and renewed energy inflation concerns weighed on valuations.

Volatility remained elevated throughout the week. While the VIX briefly dropped when risk sentiment improved, the overall tone in equity markets suggests investors are becoming more cautious as the macro implications of higher energy prices become clearer.

FX

Currency markets reflected the shift toward tighter global financial conditions. The US dollar strengthened during the week as rising US yields and geopolitical uncertainty increased demand for dollar assets.

EURUSD weakened modestly while USDJPY remained close to the high-150s as rate differentials continue to favour the US. Compared with the large swings in commodities, FX markets remain relatively stable.

What to watch next week

The key variable remains the stability of shipping routes and energy infrastructure in the Gulf. Markets will be watching closely whether tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz normalises or whether disruptions persist.

If oil prices remain elevated, investors may increasingly focus on the inflation implications for monetary policy. Higher energy prices could delay expected rate cuts and reinforce the current tightening in global financial conditions.
