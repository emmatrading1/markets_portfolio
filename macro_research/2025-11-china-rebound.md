**P/E ratio**

$$

**#1 method for investors to value stocks**

***Price is what you pay for the share and Earnings is how much profit the company makes per share***

**Example:**

- **Company earns $10/share in profit**
- **Stock trades at $100/share**
- **P/E = 100 ÷ 10 = 10x**

**What does "10x P/E" mean?**

- **You're paying $10 for every $1 of earnings**
- **Or: it takes 10 years of earnings to pay back your investment (if earnings stay flat)**

**Why does the HSI "8.5x" matter?**

- **Low P/E = cheap (market doesn't believe in growth)**
- **High P/E = expensive (market expects strong growth)**

**What does "mispriced" mean?**

**P/B Ratio**

$$
Market price per share/Book value per share
$$

***Book value = what the bank owns - what the bank owes (net assets on balance sheet)***

**Example:**

- **Bank has CNY 100 billion in assets (cash, loans, buildings)**
- **Bank owes CNY 60 billion (deposits, debts)**
- **Book value = 100 - 60 = CNY 40 billion**
- **Stock market values the bank at CNY 16 billion**
- **P/B ratio = 16 ÷ 40 = 0.4x**

### **What does 0.4x P/B mean?**

**Market saying: "Even though your balance sheet says you're worth CNY 40bn, we only think you're worth CNY 16bn (40% of book value)"**

**Why so low?**

- **Market fears banks have hidden NPLs (bad loans not disclosed)**
- **Property crisis could wipe out assets**
- **Don't trust the book value**

**Why "deep value"?**

- **Historically, banks trade at 0.8-1.2x P/B**
- **At 0.4x = market is VERY pessimistic**
- **If NPL fears go away (debt swap helps), banks could re-rate to 0.6-0.8x P/B = 50-100% stock price gain**

**My argument: Debt swap reduces NPL risk → banks safer than market thinks → 0.4x too cheap.**
