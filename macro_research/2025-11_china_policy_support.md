China Macro Pulse — Early Signs of Stabilisation After Policy Support

Week of Nov 3–7, 2025  

Thesis

China’s stimulus measures may finally be starting to show up in the data. Liquidity easing, fiscal support and targeted consumption programs are beginning to stabilise activity, although the property sector remains a significant drag.  

Despite these early signs, the Hang Seng still trades around 8.5x forward earnings, well below historical averages, suggesting investors remain skeptical that stimulus will translate into a durable recovery.

Recent developments

Oct 12 – The PBOC cut the reserve requirement ratio by 50bp, injecting roughly CNY 1tn of liquidity into the system.  
The Hang Seng gained about +2.1% on the announcement while mainland equities rallied more strongly.

Oct 28 – China’s National People’s Congress approved a CNY 10tn local government debt swap program.  
Initial market reaction was negative, as investors had hoped for fresh stimulus, but sentiment improved once the program was seen as reducing pressure on local government balance sheets.

Nov 1 – Authorities announced a CNY 300bn trade-in program for consumer goods such as EVs and appliances.  
Consumer names reacted positively, with Alibaba and JD rising on expectations of stronger retail demand.

Nov 3 – October economic data came in stronger than expected.

Retail sales: +4.8% YoY (vs +4.0% expected)  
Industrial production: +5.6% YoY  

Property activity remains weak, although the pace of decline appears to be moderating.

The Hang Seng closed around 26,100 (+1.8% on the session).

Interpretation

The data suggests that policy support may finally be feeding through to consumption. Confidence indicators have also improved slightly, although the property sector continues to weigh on overall growth.

Markets remain cautious for now, which explains why valuations are still depressed despite improving data.

Market implications

If consumption momentum continues, Chinese equities could see some rerating from current valuation levels.

Consumer platforms such as Alibaba and JD would likely benefit directly from trade-in incentives and stronger retail activity.

Banks may also see improved sentiment as the debt swap program reduces pressure on local government finances.

Key risk

The property sector remains the main downside risk. A renewed deterioration there could quickly reverse the improvement in market sentiment.

Levels to watch

HSI: support 24,500 / resistance 27,500  
USD/CNH: sustained move below 7.10 would signal improving confidence  
China 10Y yield: around 2.15%, continued declines would confirm ongoing easing
