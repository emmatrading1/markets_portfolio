# Market Pulse 2026-03-26

Driver  
Markets are driven by a renewed inflation-risk narrative as oil rebounds and supply disruption fears remain unresolved. The market is no longer only reacting to de-escalation headlines, but to the persistence of the energy shock and to the idea that political rhetoric may cap oil only temporarily. This keeps the backdrop headline-driven, unstable and still biased toward higher-for-longer pricing.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.928 (+4bp), US 10Y 4.376 (+4bp) — yields move higher again with a slight bear steepening, showing renewed inflation-risk premium across the curve rather than a clean repricing of immediate central bank action.<br>
USD: DXY 99.64 (+0.01%) — dollar remains firm but stable, suggesting elevated yields still support USD even if positioning is no longer extending aggressively.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.03%, USDJPY flat, USDCNH +0.11% — FX is relatively muted; EUR gains marginally, JPY does not strengthen despite higher volatility, and CNH remains stable, showing that broad conviction is limited outside oil and rates.<br>
Equities: SPX +0.54%, VIX 27.07 (+6.87%) — equities rise modestly but volatility also rises, highlighting a fragile move where investors still want upside exposure while continuing to hedge geopolitical and inflation risk.<br>
Commodities: Oil 92.78 (+1.62%), Gold −1.46% — oil rebounds as supply concerns persist, while gold falls further under pressure from higher yields and continued liquidation rather than behaving like a clean safe haven.

WSJ/FT Insights  
The market remains trapped in a politically driven energy regime. Oil pricing is increasingly shaped by expectations of US rhetorical intervention whenever prices rise too far, but physical disruption risk is still present underneath. At the same time, gold’s weakness shows that in this environment investors are still selling liquid winners to meet stress elsewhere, while corporate issuers are rushing to pre-fund whenever a funding window opens, reinforcing the idea that stability is tactical rather than structural.

Risk  
If oil keeps grinding higher and the Strait of Hormuz disruption persists, inflation expectations could reprice further and push yields meaningfully higher, especially in the long end. That would pressure both equities and duration again while keeping volatility elevated. On the other hand, a credible easing in supply risk would likely pull yields and oil lower together, but for now the market still looks too fragile to price a clean normalization scenario.
