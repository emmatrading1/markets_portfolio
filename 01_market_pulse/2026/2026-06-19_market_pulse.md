# Market Pulse 2026-06-19

Driver
Markets remain in the same post-ceasefire regime. Investors continue to price lower energy risk, resilient US growth and a strong AI-driven investment cycle. The focus is gradually shifting away from the Iran conflict and back toward monetary policy, economic data and the strength of the US dollar.

Transmission
Rates: US 2Y 4.18, US 10Y 4.46 — bull flattening as the 10Y falls more than the 2Y.
USD: DXY 100.79 (-0.03%) — little movement on the day, but the dollar remains strong and above 100.
FX: EURUSD flat, USDJPY lower, USDCNH higher — FX remains relatively calm overall.
Equities: SPX +1.08%, VIX 16.84 (+2.7%) — equities continue to rally despite a slight increase in volatility.
Commodities: Oil 75.7 (+0.3%), Gold -1.3% — oil is stabilising after its sharp decline while gold continues to lose safe-haven demand.

WSJ/FT Insights
The dollar story remains one of US exceptionalism. Investors continue to rebuild bullish dollar positions as strong economic data, resilient labour markets and AI-related investment support the US outlook and reduce expectations for Fed easing.

The Hormuz agreement has reduced immediate energy fears, but shipping companies remain cautious. Negotiations over the future administration of the strait, including possible maritime service fees and new operating rules, suggest that the physical energy system is not fully back to normal.

The Bank of England kept rates unchanged at 3.75%, arguing that lower oil prices have eased inflation risks, though policymakers remain cautious about second-round inflation effects.

Risk
The main risk is that markets continue to price a smooth return to normality. While sentiment has improved significantly, uncertainty remains around the implementation of the Hormuz agreement, future shipping rules and the persistence of inflation. Strong US growth could also keep central banks cautious and delay future rate cuts.
