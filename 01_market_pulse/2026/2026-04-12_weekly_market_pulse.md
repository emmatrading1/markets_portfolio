Week of Apr 7–11, 2026

The main story this week was the market stepping back from extreme fear, but not fully relaxing. The ceasefire triggered a strong move (lower oil, higher equities), but as the week went on it became clear that the underlying issues — especially around energy supply — are still very much there.

Energy

Oil was the cleanest move of the week. Big drop early on, then stabilization. But what matters is that the physical market still looks tight. Traffic through Hormuz is still very low, and supply chains haven’t normalized.

So even if prices are off the highs, the situation still looks like a supply problem — not just a volatility spike.

Rates

Rates followed the same pattern: lower at first, then a bit higher again. The key reason is inflation. The latest data came in strong, and that reminded the market that even if oil stabilizes, the impact is already feeding through.

So we’re back in that uncomfortable spot: growth slowing a bit, but inflation not really going away.

Equities and volatility

Equities had a very strong week, probably the best since the start of the conflict. But it didn’t feel like a full “risk-on” reset. Markets were still cautious into the weekend, and very dependent on headlines.

Volatility dropped, but not back to normal levels.

FX

Dollar weakened slightly, mostly because safe-haven demand faded. But FX overall still feels secondary — everything is being driven by oil and rates right now.

Credit / Private Credit

One thing that’s starting to come up more is private credit. It’s not directly linked to the energy story, but it matters in this environment.

The issue is mainly around older loans (low rates, aggressive assumptions) that now need refinancing in a much tougher environment. Not a systemic problem at this stage, but clearly something the market is starting to pay attention to.

What to watch next week

Next week is interesting because markets might try to shift focus back to fundamentals with earnings season starting.

But realistically, energy is still the key. If oil stays under control, markets can stabilize. If it moves higher again, everything (rates, equities, volatility) will react quickly.

Also worth watching: whether credit stress starts to show more clearly — especially in private markets — as conditions tighten.
