# Market Pulse 2026-03-20

Driver  
Markets are driven by a clear escalation of the energy shock following direct damage to Qatar’s Ras Laffan LNG complex. The attack raises the risk of a prolonged disruption to global gas supply, reinforcing the view that the shock is structural rather than temporary. This is pushing inflation expectations higher and tightening constraints on central banks globally.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.856 (+6bp), US 10Y 4.293 (+4bp) — front-end repricing dominates, with a continued bear flattening as markets adjust to sustained inflation pressure and reduced probability of rate cuts.<br>
USD: DXY 99.43 (+0.27%) — dollar strengthens alongside higher yields, supported by rate differentials and demand for liquidity in a stressed environment.<br>
FX: EURUSD −0.25%, USDJPY +0.64%, USDCNH +0.10% — USD strength broad-based, with yen weakening in line with higher US yields and CNH under mild pressure reflecting global risk sensitivity.<br>
Equities: SPX −0.28%, VIX 24.54 (+2.00%) — equities remain under pressure and volatility elevated, consistent with a controlled risk-off driven by higher rates and inflation concerns.<br>
Commodities: Oil 95.48 (+0.93%), Gold +0.59% — oil remains structurally elevated as supply risks persist, while gold edges higher on geopolitical demand despite upward pressure from yields.

FT Insights  
Markets are increasingly pricing a protracted energy shock, with higher oil and gas prices feeding into inflation expectations and pushing central banks toward a more constrained policy stance. Rate cuts are being repriced out, and the risk of renewed tightening is no longer negligible as policymakers seek to contain second-round inflation effects.

Risk  
Further escalation targeting energy infrastructure could extend supply disruptions and entrench inflation, forcing central banks to maintain restrictive policy for longer. This would sustain upward pressure on front-end yields, weigh on equities and keep volatility elevated across asset classes.
