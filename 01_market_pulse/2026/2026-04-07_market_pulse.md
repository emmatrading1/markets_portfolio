# Market Pulse 2026-04-07

Driver  
Markets are transitioning from pure geopolitical risk to structural repricing of energy vulnerability and long-term supply chains. The focus is shifting from short-term oil spikes to a broader regime where energy security, infrastructure and policy constraints drive macro expectations.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.84 (−1bp), US 10Y 4.32 (−1.4bp) — yields move lower with a bull flattening, suggesting growth concerns starting to re-emerge despite elevated energy prices.<br>
USD: DXY 99.82 (−0.17%) — dollar slightly weaker but remains at elevated levels, still supported by its safe-haven status.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.27%, USDJPY −0.08%, USDCNH −0.27% — FX relatively stable, reflecting lack of strong directional conviction beyond mild USD softening.<br>
Equities: SPX +0.44%, VIX 24.55 (+1.57%) — equities hold up while volatility rises, pointing to cautious risk-taking rather than clean risk-on.<br>
Commodities: Oil 111.41 (−1.06%), Gold +0.71% — oil consolidates at very high levels, confirming the persistence of the shock rather than a reversal.

WSJ/FT Insights  
FT highlights that the Hormuz crisis is no longer just a disruption but a catalyst for a structural redesign of global energy flows. Gulf countries are actively shifting from reliance on maritime chokepoints to pipeline networks and alternative corridors, while Europe faces repeated energy-driven inflation shocks that complicate monetary policy. The key takeaway is that the market is beginning to price not just current disruption, but a long-term reconfiguration of trade, energy infrastructure and geopolitical dependencies.

Risk  
If the energy shock becomes embedded into long-term infrastructure shifts, markets may continue repricing through higher structural inflation and persistent volatility. In the short term, however, the decline in yields suggests that growth concerns could temporarily dominate, creating a more mixed regime between stagflation risk and cyclical slowdown.
