# Market Pulse 2026-03-25

Driver  
Markets are driven by cautious hopes of de-escalation in the US–Iran conflict, with ceasefire discussions and tentative signals around the Strait of Hormuz reducing immediate energy shock fears. This is pushing oil lower and allowing part of the previous inflation-risk repricing to unwind, but the broader macro backdrop remains fragile and headline-sensitive.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.867 (−4bp), US 10Y 4.332 (−4bp) — bull steepening as front-end yields retrace more clearly, reflecting a partial unwind of extreme higher-for-longer pricing as immediate inflation tail risks ease.<br>
USD: DXY 99.33 (+0.15%) — dollar remains firm despite lower yields, showing that safe-haven demand and relative US resilience are still supporting the currency.<br>
FX: EURUSD −0.07%, USDJPY +0.17%, USDCNH +0.15% — USD strength persists overall; JPY weakness despite lower yields suggests positioning and policy divergence still matter more than the pure rates move, while CNH remains relatively stable.<br>
Equities: SPX −0.37%, VIX 25.71 (−4.60%) — equities give back part of the relief move, but falling volatility shows tail-risk pricing is easing even if markets are not fully back in risk-on mode.<br>
Commodities: Oil 87.55 (−0.94%), Gold +1.91% — oil continues to correct as supply disruption fears soften, while gold rises sharply as investors still seek protection against unresolved geopolitical and policy uncertainty.

WSJ/FT Insights  
The market is moving from pure inflation-shock panic toward a more balanced but still unstable regime driven by geopolitical headlines. Lower oil and falling yields suggest some reversal of the recent extreme higher-for-longer repricing, but firm USD and strong gold show investors are not ready to abandon defensive positioning. The key point is that de-escalation is improving sentiment at the margin, not yet restoring a clean macro outlook.

Risk  
If negotiations stall and the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively constrained, oil could quickly reprice higher and revive the inflation shock narrative. That would push yields back up, restore pressure on equities and lift volatility again. Conversely, a credible and lasting de-escalation would allow further easing in yields, lower energy prices and a more durable recovery in risk sentiment.
