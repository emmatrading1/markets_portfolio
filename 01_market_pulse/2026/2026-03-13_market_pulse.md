# Market Pulse 2026-03-13

Driver  
Oil markets remain the dominant macro driver as disruption risks around the Strait of Hormuz continue to shape investor expectations. While policymakers attempt to stabilise supply through measures such as easing restrictions on stranded Russian cargo and discussing escorted tanker routes, markets remain focused on the possibility that elevated energy prices could push inflation higher and keep global monetary policy restrictive for longer.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.742 (+0.08%), US 10Y 4.273 (+0.28%) — long-end yields rise faster than the front end, steepening the curve and suggesting markets are adding inflation and term premium rather than purely repricing short-term Fed policy expectations.<br>
USD: DXY 99.343 (+0.42%) — the dollar strengthens for a third session as higher yields and tighter global financial conditions support demand for USD assets.<br>
FX: EURUSD −0.47%, USDJPY +0.10%, USDCNH +0.24% — broad USD strength across major pairs as widening rate differentials favour the US and global risk sentiment weakens.<br>
Equities: SPX −1.52%, VIX 26.78 (−1.87%) — equities fall as higher yields and persistent energy inflation risks pressure valuations, while the drop in VIX suggests repricing rather than a fresh volatility shock.<br>
Commodities: Oil 95.08 (−1.35%), Gold +0.29% — oil remains elevated as markets continue to price supply disruption risk in the Middle East while gold edges higher on safe-haven demand despite the stronger dollar.

FT Insights  
Markets are rapidly repricing the outlook for US monetary policy as higher petrol prices force investors to reconsider how quickly the Federal Reserve can ease policy. With energy costs surging, traders have pushed expectations for rate cuts further into the future, reflecting concern that inflation could remain elevated even if growth slows.

Risk  
If disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz persists or escalates, oil prices could move significantly higher and push the market toward a clearer stagflation regime characterised by higher long-term yields, a stronger dollar and sustained pressure on global equities.
