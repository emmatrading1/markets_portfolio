# Market Pulse 2026-03-19

Driver  
Markets remain driven by the oil shock stemming from the disruption of flows through the Strait of Hormuz. Physical oil markets are experiencing acute scarcity, with regional crude prices surging and a growing dislocation between physical and futures pricing. At the same time, the Federal Reserve has acknowledged that higher energy prices will push inflation higher, reinforcing uncertainty around the policy outlook.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.824 (+5bp), US 10Y 4.291 (+3bp) — front-end yields rise more than the long end, resulting in a bear flattening as markets reprice higher-for-longer policy rates in response to persistent energy-driven inflation.<br>
USD: DXY 100.17 (−0.05%) — the dollar remains broadly stable despite higher yields, suggesting positioning effects and uncertainty around the durability of tighter financial conditions.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.07%, USDJPY −0.35%, USDCNH +0.04% — mixed USD performance, with yen strength reflecting safe-haven demand in a risk-off environment despite rising US yields.<br>
Equities: SPX −1.36%, VIX 25.71 (+2.47%) — equities decline and volatility rises, indicating a clear shift toward risk-off as higher yields and inflation risks weigh on valuations.<br>
Commodities: Oil 98.40 (−0.65%), Gold −2.26% — oil remains elevated despite a modest pullback, while gold declines as higher real yields and a still-strong dollar reduce its appeal.

FT Insights  
The Federal Reserve is increasingly constrained by the inflationary impact of the energy shock, with policymakers signalling that higher oil prices could delay rate cuts and potentially even reopen the discussion around further tightening. Markets have pushed expectations for easing further out, reflecting a shift toward a prolonged restrictive policy stance.

Risk  
A prolonged disruption in oil supply could sustain elevated inflation and force central banks to maintain restrictive policy for longer, increasing the risk of a policy-driven slowdown. This would likely reinforce higher front-end yields, pressure equities further, and increase volatility across asset classes.
