# Weekend Macro Brief — March 7 2026

Driver  
Escalating Middle East conflict raising risks of a global energy supply shock.

Macro Developments  
Iranian drone strikes targeted energy infrastructure across the Gulf, including Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery and Qatar’s Ras Laffan LNG facilities. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has collapsed, with tanker traffic down roughly 90% and insurance costs surging. The region accounts for a significant share of global oil, LNG and fertiliser exports, raising concerns about disruptions to global energy and commodity supply chains.

Transmission  
Energy: Brent crude has surged from ~$72 before the conflict to around $92 as markets price potential supply shortages. LNG disruptions in Qatar, which accounts for roughly one-fifth of global LNG supply, risk tightening global gas markets.

Rates: Rising energy prices are pushing inflation expectations higher, triggering a broad sell-off in global bond markets. UK 10Y gilt yields rose sharply to 4.62%, while the US 10Y Treasury moved toward 4.13%.

Policy expectations: Interest rate swaps have repriced aggressively. Markets that previously expected multiple rate cuts from central banks are now pricing far fewer, with some contracts even implying the possibility of further tightening in Europe.

Market Narrative  
Markets are shifting from a “rate cuts” narrative to a renewed inflation risk scenario. The energy shock is forcing investors to reconsider how quickly central banks can ease policy, particularly if higher oil and gas prices feed through to headline inflation.

Key Points to Watch  
• Brent crude approaching $100 would significantly amplify inflation concerns  
• Stability of shipping flows through the Strait of Hormuz  
• Inflation expectations and front-end rate repricing  
• Central bank communication on energy-driven inflation shocks
