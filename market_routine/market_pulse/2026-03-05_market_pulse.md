# Market Pulse 2026-03-05

Driver

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East continue to drive energy supply concerns, while China signals slower but stable growth with a 4.5–5% GDP target. Markets are balancing inflation risks from higher energy prices with expectations that the Iran conflict may remain relatively short-lived.

Transmission

Rates: US 2Y 3.56%, US 10Y 4.12% (+~0.5%) — yields move higher as markets price inflation risk from energy prices and geopolitical uncertainty.

USD: DXY 98.91 (+0.14%) — dollar slightly stronger supported by higher US yields.

FX: EURUSD 1.162 (-0.10%), USDJPY 157.16 (+0.07%), USDCNH 6.89 (-0.02%).

Equities: SPX 6869 (+0.78%), VIX 21.19 (+0.19%) — equities resilient but volatility ticking higher as investors continue hedging geopolitical risk.

Commodities: Oil +0.08% (Middle East supply concerns), Gold +0.43% (safe-haven demand).

FT Insights

China set a GDP growth target of 4.5–5% for 2026, slightly lower than last year’s goal, reflecting domestic economic challenges and rising geopolitical risks. Authorities signalled continued fiscal support but emphasised long-term technological development rather than aggressive short-term stimulus.

Energy markets are reacting strongly to the Iran conflict. European gas prices have surged more than 50%, pushing utilities in Europe and northeast Asia to switch to coal. Thermal coal prices have risen around 26% to their highest levels in more than two years as power producers seek alternatives to expensive gas.

Despite inflation risks from higher energy prices, rate markets appear to be pricing the conflict as a short-term disruption rather than a prolonged macro shock.

Risk

A prolonged disruption to LNG supply or escalation around the Strait of Hormuz could push energy prices significantly higher, reinforcing inflation pressures and delaying global monetary easing.
