# Market Pulse 2026-03-23

Driver  
Markets continue to reprice the persistence of the energy shock, with the focus shifting from a temporary supply disruption to sustained inflation pressure. The key dynamic is now the transmission into rate expectations, as investors move away from rate cuts and begin to price a higher-for-longer regime with some probability of renewed tightening.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.969 (+6bp), US 10Y 4.419 (+3bp) — bear flattening continues as front-end yields lead the move, reflecting a repricing of Fed expectations toward fewer cuts and potential hikes.<br>
USD: DXY 99.91 (+0.41%) — dollar strengthens on higher yields, safe-haven demand, and relative US resilience as an energy exporter.<br>
FX: EURUSD −0.47%, USDJPY +0.17%, USDCNH +0.09% — broad USD strength, with JPY weakening alongside rising yields and CNH under pressure in a risk-off environment.<br>
Equities: SPX −1.51%, VIX 29.79 (+11.24%) — equities decline sharply and volatility rises toward stress levels, reflecting a more pronounced risk-off driven by higher rates and macro uncertainty.<br>
Commodities: Oil 98.91 (+0.83%), Gold −4.09% — oil remains elevated as markets price prolonged supply disruption, while gold falls sharply under pressure from rising real yields, USD strength and position unwinds.

FT Insights  
The energy shock is increasingly feeding into global rate expectations rather than remaining confined to commodities. Markets are reassessing central bank reaction functions, with inflation persistence limiting policy flexibility. The US continues to outperform due to structural advantages, reinforcing dollar strength and relative market resilience.

Risk  
A sustained rise in oil above $100 could further entrench inflation expectations and accelerate the repricing toward tighter policy. This would push front-end yields higher, deepen pressure on equities, and maintain elevated volatility, with particular vulnerability in energy-importing economies.
