# Market Pulse 2026-03-11

Driver
Energy markets remain the central macro driver as the Middle East conflict continues to disrupt oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries roughly 20% of global oil supply. Governments are now considering a coordinated release of strategic oil reserves to stabilise markets after extreme volatility pushed crude close to $120 before reversing sharply.

Transmission
Rates: US 2Y ↑0.20%, US 10Y ↑0.26% — curve steepening as long-end yields rise more than the front end, reflecting persistent inflation risks linked to the energy shock.
USD: DXY 99.02 (+0.11%) — stronger dollar supported by rising US yields.
FX: EURUSD ↓0.12%, USDJPY ↑0.25%, USDCNH ↓0.15% — broad USD strength while CNH remains relatively stable.
Equities: SPX ↓0.21%, VIX 26.23 (+5.21%) — mild risk-off tone as investors hedge geopolitical uncertainty.
Commodities: Oil $87.27 (+1%), Gold +0.08% — oil stabilising after extreme swings earlier in the week while gold remains supported by safe-haven demand.

FT Insights
Oil markets experienced one of the most volatile sessions on record, with Brent swinging from $119 to $84 within hours as traders reacted to geopolitical developments and the potential release of emergency reserves. Market makers reported spreads widening sharply as liquidity deteriorated and speculative positions were rapidly unwound.

Risk
The key uncertainty remains the duration and escalation of the Middle East conflict. Any disruption to Gulf energy infrastructure or shipping routes could quickly restore the geopolitical risk premium in oil and push inflation expectations higher again.
