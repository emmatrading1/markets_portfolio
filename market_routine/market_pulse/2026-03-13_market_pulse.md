# Market Pulse 2026-03-13

Driver
Oil remains the dominant macro driver, but today’s cross-asset move is sharper than yesterday: markets are pricing a more persistent inflation shock from disrupted energy flows while also starting to worry about the longer-term growth damage. The key theme is no longer just geopolitical uncertainty, but a more explicit stagflation concern.

Transmission
Rates: US 2Y +0.08%, US 10Y +0.28% — intraday curve steepening as the long end sells off more, pointing to higher term premium and inflation-risk premium rather than a pure repricing of near-term Fed policy.  
USD: DXY 99.343 (+0.42%) — stronger dollar as higher yields and oil-driven inflation fears support USD.  
FX: EURUSD −0.47%, USDJPY +0.10%, USDCNH +0.24% — broad USD strength, consistent with higher US yields and tighter global financial conditions.  
Equities: SPX −1.52%, VIX 26.78 (−1.87%) — equities weaken materially, but the fall in VIX suggests repricing and growth pressure rather than a fresh volatility panic.  
Commodities: Oil 95.08 (screen −1.35%), Gold +0.29% — oil remains high despite intraday noise, while gold is supported by safe-haven demand even as USD and yields rise.

FT Insights
The main market shift is the rapid pricing-out of Fed cuts as higher petrol prices push investors to rethink the inflation outlook. At the same time, the broader macro backdrop is becoming more complex: China’s renminbi infrastructure push matters less because it replaces the dollar immediately and more because it may slowly raise the marginal cost of staying inside the dollar funding system.

Risk
If oil stays elevated and the Strait of Hormuz remains disrupted, the market is likely to move further toward a stagflation regime: fewer Fed cuts, higher long-end yields, a firmer dollar and weaker equities.
