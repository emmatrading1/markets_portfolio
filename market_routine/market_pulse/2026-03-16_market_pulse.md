# Market Pulse 2026-03-16

Driver  
Markets are temporarily unwinding part of last week’s geopolitical and inflation premium as oil prices retreat and risk sentiment improves, even though the underlying Middle East conflict remains unresolved. The market narrative today is less about a fresh escalation and more about a partial reversal of the extreme hedging and repricing that had built up around a sustained Strait of Hormuz supply shock.

Transmission  
Rates: US 2Y 3.694 (−0.94%), US 10Y 4.234 (−1.14%) — both Treasury yields fall as investors buy duration and partially remove the inflation premium priced in earlier in the week, with the long end declining slightly more as fears of an immediate energy-driven policy shock ease.<br>
USD: DXY 99.856 (−0.63%) — the dollar weakens as lower US yields reduce rate support and part of the recent safe-haven demand fades.<br>
FX: EURUSD +0.73%, USDJPY −0.34%, USDCNH −0.24% — broad USD softness across major pairs as falling yields and a modest improvement in risk sentiment weigh on the dollar.<br>
Equities: SPX +1.14%, VIX 24.19 (−11.03%) — equities rebound as investors unwind defensive positioning, while the sharp drop in VIX suggests a meaningful reversal in hedging demand rather than a market pricing a new immediate shock.<br>
Commodities: Oil 95.46 (−3.87%), Gold −0.26% — oil gives back part of last week’s geopolitical premium as markets reassess the probability of a sustained supply disruption, while gold slips modestly as investors rotate away from safe-haven assets despite the weaker dollar.

FT Insights  
The broader macro issue is shifting from the immediate market reaction to the policy implications of a prolonged energy shock. Central banks are unlikely to react immediately, but they also cannot afford to dismiss renewed inflation pressure after the policy mistakes of 2022. At the same time, the BIS is warning that if the conflict lasts longer, higher rates, weaker asset prices and stress in private credit could amplify the macro damage well beyond the oil market itself.

Risk  
The key uncertainty remains the duration of the conflict and the stability of shipping flows through the Strait of Hormuz. If oil resumes its climb toward or above $100 and stays there, markets could quickly move back toward a stagflation-style repricing with higher long-term yields, renewed dollar strength and fresh pressure on global risk assets.
