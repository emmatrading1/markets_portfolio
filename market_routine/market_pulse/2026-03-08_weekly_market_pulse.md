Week of Mar 3–7, 2026  

The dominant theme this week was the escalation of the conflict involving Iran and the resulting shock to energy markets. Oil prices surged as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz became severely disrupted, raising concerns about a potential supply shock in global energy markets.

Energy

Brent crude moved sharply higher during the week and finished around $91 per barrel. The key issue is the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of global oil and LNG usually transit. Even if the strait is not fully closed for long, slower tanker movements and partial disruptions could still remove around 2mn barrels per day from global supply.

Markets are therefore trying to price the probability of a sustained supply disruption. A prolonged closure of the strait would have far more dramatic consequences, but for now investors appear to assume the disruption will be temporary.

Rates

Bond markets reacted mainly through the inflation channel. Higher oil and gas prices pushed inflation expectations higher, which kept pressure on sovereign bonds.

The US 10Y yield ended the week around 4.14%, while shorter-term yields moved slightly lower. The overall move in rates reflects uncertainty about whether central banks will prioritise inflation risks from energy prices or the softer economic data that has started to appear in the US.

Equities and volatility

Equities weakened modestly during the week. The S&P 500 finished down about 1.3% while volatility jumped sharply, with the VIX rising above 29.

The move does not yet look like panic. Many investors appear to be trimming positions that performed well earlier this year rather than aggressively de-risking portfolios.

FX

The dollar weakened slightly during the week, with DXY below 99. EURUSD moved modestly higher while USDJPY continued to trade near 158.

FX markets remain relatively calm compared with the moves in commodities and volatility.

Power markets

Energy market stress is also showing up in European electricity prices. Gas disruptions linked to the conflict have increased the cost of power generation, while the growing share of solar power is creating large intraday swings in electricity prices.

Day-ahead power prices in parts of Europe have been moving by several hundred euros per megawatt hour within a few hours as solar generation drops in the evening and gas plants return to the grid.

What to watch next week

The key variable remains the evolution of the conflict and whether energy infrastructure or shipping routes in the Gulf continue to be disrupted.

Markets will also be watching whether higher oil prices begin to feed more directly into inflation expectations and rate pricing.
